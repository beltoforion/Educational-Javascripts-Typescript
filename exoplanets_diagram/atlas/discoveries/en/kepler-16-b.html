September 15, 2011

Kepler-16b: first planet orbiting two stars

NASA's Kepler space telescope delivered the first unambiguous detection of a planet orbiting two stars. Seen from Earth, Kepler-16b passes in front of both stars, which also eclipse each other. This allowed the dimensions of all three bodies to be determined precisely.

The planet resembles Saturn in mass and size and orbits the stellar pair in 229 days on a nearly circular path. The stars have 69 and 20 percent of the Sun's mass and orbit each other in 41 days. All orbits lie nearly in one plane, which suggests that the planet formed in a disk around both stars. The system is about 200 light-years away.

Sources: NASA/JPL: "NASA's Kepler Discovery Confirms First Planet Orbiting Two Stars", 2011-09-15; arXiv: "Kepler-16: A Transiting Circumbinary Planet" (Doyle et al.), 2011-09-15

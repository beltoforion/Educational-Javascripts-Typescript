PSR J2322-2650 b: a pulsar planet with a carbon atmosphere

The James Webb Space Telescope has found a type of atmosphere never observed before on the planet PSR J2322-2650 b: it is dominated by helium and carbon. Instead of water, methane or carbon dioxide, the carbon molecules C2 and C3 showed up.

The planet has about one Jupiter mass and orbits a pulsar in just 7.8 hours, at a distance of about 1.6 million kilometers. According to models, the pulsar's gravity pulls it into a lemon-like shape. How such a carbon-rich planet could have formed is unexplained. Deep inside, the carbon could be squeezed into diamond under high pressure.

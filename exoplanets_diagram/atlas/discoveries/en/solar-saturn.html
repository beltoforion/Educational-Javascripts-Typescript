Our Solar System

Saturn: the ringed planet of low density

Saturn is the second-largest planet in the Solar System, a gas giant of hydrogen and helium with 9.45 Earth radii and 95 Earth masses. It orbits the Sun in 29.4 years at 9.6 AU. Its mean density of 687 kg/m³ is lower than that of water. Its rings consist of billions of chunks of ice and rock.

Exoplanets the size of Saturn or Jupiter and larger count as gas giants. One example of about Saturn's mass is WASP-39 b: orbiting very close to its star, it is about 900 °C hot and puffed up to 1.3 times Jupiter's diameter. The Solar System's gas giants, by contrast, are cool and compact.

Moons: Saturn has 293 confirmed moons (as of August 2026), far more than any other planet. Titan, the largest, is bigger than Mercury and the only moon with a thick atmosphere; lakes and seas of liquid methane and ethane lie on its surface. Enceladus, only about 500 km across, spews jets of water vapor and ice particles; they come from an ocean beneath its icy shell and form Saturn's E ring.

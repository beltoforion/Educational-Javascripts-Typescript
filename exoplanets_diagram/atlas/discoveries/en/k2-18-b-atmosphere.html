September 11, 2023

Methane and carbon dioxide in the atmosphere of K2-18 b

K2-18 b is 8.6 times as massive as Earth and orbits a red dwarf 120 light-years away within its habitable zone. The James Webb Space Telescope has detected methane and carbon dioxide in its atmosphere. The researchers therefore consider it possible that K2-18 b is a so-called "Hycean planet" with a hydrogen-rich atmosphere and a global ocean. In addition, they found a weak signal that could originate from dimethyl sulfide, a molecule that on Earth is produced almost exclusively by living organisms.

In April 2025, the same team again reported hints of dimethyl sulfide based on further Webb data. Independent analyses, however, found no robust evidence for it. As things stand today, the signal is not an indication of life.

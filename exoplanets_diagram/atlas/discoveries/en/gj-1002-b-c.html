GJ 1002: two Earth-mass planets in the habitable zone

Using the ESPRESSO and CARMENES spectrographs, a team led by the Instituto de Astrofísica de Canarias discovered two planets orbiting the red dwarf GJ 1002. The star is less than 16 light-years away and has barely one-eighth of the Sun's mass.

Both planets have masses similar to Earth's and lie in the habitable zone of the star. GJ 1002 b takes a little more than 10 days for one orbit, GJ 1002 c about twice as long. Because the system is so close, both are considered good candidates for future studies of their atmospheres.

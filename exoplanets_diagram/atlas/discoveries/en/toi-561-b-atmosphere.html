TOI-561 b: strongest evidence yet for an atmosphere on a rocky planet

The James Webb Space Telescope has measured the dayside of the ultra-hot super-Earth TOI-561 b. As a bare rock it should be almost 2,700 degrees Celsius hot, but the measurements indicate closer to 1,800 degrees. In the team's view this is best explained by a thick atmosphere of volatile substances above a global magma ocean.

TOI-561 b has about 1.4 times the radius of Earth and orbits its star in less than 11 hours. The star is about twice as old as the Sun and poor in iron. NASA speaks of the strongest evidence yet for an atmosphere on a rocky planet outside the solar system. The exact composition is still open.

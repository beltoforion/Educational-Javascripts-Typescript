Kepler-13 Ab: titanium oxide snow on the nightside

Kepler-13 Ab is one of the hottest known hot Jupiters: its dayside is more than 2,700 degrees Celsius hot. According to ESA/Hubble, the planet is about six times as massive as Jupiter and always keeps the same side facing its star.

According to Hubble observations, the strong gravity on the nightside pulls titanium oxide downward, where it precipitates as snow. It is the first detection of such a "cold trap" on an exoplanet. Because titanium oxide is missing on the dayside, the atmosphere there grows colder with increasing altitude. The system is 1,730 light-years away.

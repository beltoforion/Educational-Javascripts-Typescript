LTT 9779 b: metallic clouds turn a planet into a mirror

Measurements by ESA's Cheops satellite show that the planet LTT 9779 b reflects about 80 percent of the light from its star – more than Venus with about 75 percent. That makes it the most reflective exoplanet found so far.

The roughly Neptune-sized planet orbits its star in less than a day; its dayside is about 2,000 degrees Celsius hot. The high reflectivity is attributed to clouds of silicate mixed with metals such as titanium. They can form despite the heat because the atmosphere is oversaturated with silicate and metal vapor.

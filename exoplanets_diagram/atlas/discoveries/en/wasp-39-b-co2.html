First unambiguous detection of carbon dioxide in an exoplanet atmosphere

Shortly after the start of its science operations, the James Webb Space Telescope unambiguously detected carbon dioxide in the atmosphere of the "hot Saturn" WASP-39 b, about 700 light-years away. What was measured is the starlight that passes through the planet's atmosphere during a transit. The bump at 4.3 microns in the spectrum shows the absorption by carbon dioxide. Later measurements also found sulfur dioxide in the atmosphere of WASP-39 b, which is produced by photochemistry, that is, by the action of starlight.

Source: ESA/Webb: "Webb detects carbon dioxide in exoplanet atmosphere." Online; esawebb.org; 2022-08-25

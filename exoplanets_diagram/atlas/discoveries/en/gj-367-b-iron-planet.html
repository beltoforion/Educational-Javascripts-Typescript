GJ 367 b: an iron-rich planet with an eight-hour year

An international team led by the DLR Institute of Planetary Research characterized the planet GJ 367 b. It orbits its star in just under eight hours, has about half the mass of Earth and, with a diameter of just over 9,000 kilometers, is slightly larger than Mars.

The rocky planet is much denser than Earth. Its structure resembles that of Mercury, and it probably has a large iron core. On the side facing the star, temperatures reach 1,300 to 1,500 degrees Celsius – enough to melt rock and iron. The system is just under 31 light-years away.

Source: DLR: "An eight-hour year.", 2021-12-02

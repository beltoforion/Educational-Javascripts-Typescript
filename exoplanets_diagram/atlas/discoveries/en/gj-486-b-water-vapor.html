GJ 486 b: water vapor – from the planet or from its star?

The James Webb Space Telescope has found hints of water vapor at the hot rocky planet GJ 486 b. The planet is about 30 percent larger than Earth, three times as massive, and orbits its red dwarf star in less than two days. Its surface temperature is about 430 degrees Celsius.

If the water vapor comes from the planet, the planet would have an atmosphere despite the heat. However, the signal could just as well come from cool starspots on the star itself. Further observations with other instruments on the telescope are intended to clarify which explanation applies.

Source: ESA/Webb: "Webb finds water vapour, but from a rocky planet or its star?" (image page GJ486b), 2023-05-01

January 9, 1992

First planets beyond the Solar System, orbiting a pulsar

Wolszczan and Frail used the 305-meter Arecibo radio telescope to time the pulses of the millisecond pulsar PSR B1257+12, which rotates once every 6.2 milliseconds. Regular deviations showed that at least two planet-sized bodies orbit the neutron star.

Their masses are at least 2.8 and 3.4 Earth masses, and their orbital periods are 98.2 and 66.6 days. The paper appeared in Nature on January 9, 1992, years before the first planet was found around a Sun-like star. The authors put the pulsar at roughly 500 parsecs, about 1600 light-years.

Sources: Nature: "A planetary system around the millisecond pulsar PSR1257 + 12" (Wolszczan & Frail), 1992-01-09; ESO: "First Detection of Reflected Visible Light from 51 Pegasi b" (note 2: two pulsar planets predate 51 Pegasi b), 2015-04-22

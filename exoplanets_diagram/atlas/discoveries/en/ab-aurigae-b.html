Observation of a protoplanet sheds light on the mechanisms of planet formation

Through direct imaging with the Subaru telescope and the Hubble Space Telescope, researchers have discovered the youngest exoplanet known to date in the protoplanetary disk of the star system AB Aurigae. The protoplanet, which is still forming, orbits its star at a distance of 93 AU and could grow into a gas giant with nine times the mass of Jupiter.

The large distance from the star could indicate that planet formation here follows the gravitational instability model. In this model, the planetary disk first becomes unstable in its outer region, which shows itself in the formation of spiral arms. Parts of the disk then collapse under their own gravity and directly form gas giants.

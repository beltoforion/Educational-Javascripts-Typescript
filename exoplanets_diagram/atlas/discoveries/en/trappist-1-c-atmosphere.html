TRAPPIST-1 c has no thick carbon dioxide atmosphere

The MIRI instrument on the James Webb Space Telescope measured the heat radiated by the rocky planet TRAPPIST-1 c. Its dayside has a temperature of roughly 380 kelvins (about 107 degrees Celsius). That made it the coolest rocky planet characterized by its thermal emission up to that time.

TRAPPIST-1 c is about the size of Venus and receives a similar amount of radiation from its star. According to the measurements, however, a thick, Venus-like carbon dioxide atmosphere is unlikely. The data are consistent with bare rock or with a very thin atmosphere. The results were published in the journal Nature.

Sources: NASA: "Webb Rules Out Thick Carbon Dioxide Atmosphere for Rocky Exoplanet.", 2023-06-19; ESA/Webb: "TRAPPIST-1 c emission spectra." (image page), 2023-06-19

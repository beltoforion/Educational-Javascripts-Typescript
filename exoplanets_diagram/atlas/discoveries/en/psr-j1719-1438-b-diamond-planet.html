PSR J1719-1438 b: the "diamond planet" of a pulsar

A planetary-mass companion orbits the millisecond pulsar PSR J1719-1438 in just two hours and ten minutes. Its distance of 600,000 kilometers is slightly less than the radius of the Sun. The companion has a diameter of only about 60,000 kilometers but is at least as dense as platinum.

It is probably the remnant of a white dwarf that has lost more than 99.9 percent of its mass to the pulsar. It is thought to consist mainly of carbon and oxygen. At this density the material must be crystalline – a large part of it could therefore have a diamond-like structure.

HD 80606 b: an extreme orbit with a heat shock

When it was discovered in 2001, HD 80606 b had the most elongated orbit of all exoplanets known at the time: every 111 days it approaches its star from 127 million to about 5 million kilometers.

The Spitzer Space Telescope observed the planet during such a close passage. Within six hours its temperature rose from about 530 to about 1,230 degrees Celsius. It was the first time that weather changes on an exoplanet were followed in real time. Unlike most hot Jupiters, the planet is not tidally locked but rotates on its axis about every 34 hours. The system is 190 light-years away.

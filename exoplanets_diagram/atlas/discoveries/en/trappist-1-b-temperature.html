Temperature of a rocky planet measured: TRAPPIST-1 b

The planetary system TRAPPIST-1, 40 light-years away, consists of seven rocky planets roughly the size of Earth, three of which lie in the habitable zone. The MIRI instrument of the James Webb Space Telescope was used to measure the thermal emission of the innermost planet, TRAPPIST-1 b, as it disappeared behind its star. Its dayside has a temperature of about 500 K. This fits best with a planet without any significant atmosphere that could transport heat from the dayside to the nightside.

GJ 1132 b: a second atmosphere from volcanic gases? A disputed finding

Based on Hubble data, a team reported evidence of a newly formed atmosphere on the rocky planet GJ 1132 b. It was said to contain hydrogen, hydrogen cyanide, methane and ammonia and to be replenished by volcanism from the planet's interior after the planet had lost its original gas envelope.

A second analysis of five transits observed with Hubble, however, found a spectrum without recognizable features. According to it, GJ 1132 b has either an atmosphere of heavy molecules, a high-altitude haze layer, or practically no atmosphere. The planet has 1.7 Earth masses and 1.1 Earth radii.

Sources: ESA/Hubble: "Hubble Sees New Atmosphere Forming on a Rocky Exoplanet." (heic2104), 2021-03-11; arXiv: Libby-Roberts et al., "The Featureless HST/WFC3 Transmission Spectrum of the Rocky Exoplanet GJ 1132b: No Evidence For A Cloud-Free Primordial Atmosphere and Constraints on Starspot Contamination.", 2021-05-21

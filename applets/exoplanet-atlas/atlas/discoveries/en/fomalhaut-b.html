Fomalhaut b: from photographed planet to dust cloud

In November 2008, a Hubble team reported the first visible-light image of a planet: Fomalhaut b, no more than three Jupiter masses, just inside the dust ring around the star Fomalhaut, 25 light-years away. From images taken in 2004 and 2006, an orbital period of 872 years was calculated.

Later the object faded, and in Hubble images from 2014 it had vanished. An analysis presented in 2020 interprets it as an expanding dust cloud from the collision of two bodies about 200 kilometers across. In 2025, Hubble found a second such cloud nearby. Fomalhaut b is therefore no longer regarded as a planet.

Sources: ESA/Hubble: "Hubble directly observes planet orbiting Fomalhaut", 2008-11-13; ESA/Hubble: "Hubble Observes Aftermath of Massive Collision", 2020-04-20; ESA/Hubble: "Hubble sees asteroids colliding at nearby star for first time", 2025-12-18

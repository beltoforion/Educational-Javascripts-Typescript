October 1995

51 Pegasi b: first planet around a Sun-like star

Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz made the discovery of 51 Pegasi b public in October 1995; it was the first planet found around a Sun-like star. They found it at the Haute-Provence Observatory in southern France. The gas giant, about 50 light-years away, is regarded as the archetype of the "hot Jupiters": planets similar to Jupiter in size and mass that orbit very close to their star.

In 2015, ESO's HARPS instrument made the first spectroscopic detection of visible light reflected by an exoplanet, on this very planet. The result implied a mass of about half that of Jupiter. Mayor and Queloz received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2019.

Sources: Nobel Prize: "Press release: The Nobel Prize in Physics 2019", 2019-10-08; ESO: "First Detection of Reflected Visible Light from 51 Pegasi b", 2015-04-22

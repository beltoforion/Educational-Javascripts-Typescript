WD 1856 b: a giant planet that outlived its star

The Jupiter-sized planet WD 1856 b orbits a white dwarf, the remnant of a Sun-like star, every 34 hours at a distance of less than three million kilometers. The James Webb Space Telescope observed its transit and determined its mass to be between four and eleven Jupiter masses. The atmosphere showed signs of cloud particles and hydrocarbons, most likely methane.

At about 126 degrees Celsius, the planet is warmer than the light of the white dwarf can explain. The researchers conclude that it survived the red giant phase on a wide orbit and migrated inward only billions of years later. The system is about 80 light-years away.

Source: ESA/Webb: "Webb studies how a planet survived death of its star", 2026-07-01

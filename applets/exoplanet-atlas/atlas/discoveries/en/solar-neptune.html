Our Solar System

Neptune: distant ice giant and namesake of the mini-Neptunes

Neptune is the outermost planet of the Solar System, an ice giant with 3.9 Earth radii and 17.1 Earth masses. It orbits the Sun in 163.7 years at 30.2 AU; its mean temperature is −200 °C. Supersonic winds blow in its atmosphere. It is the only planet that is not visible to the naked eye.

Neptune is the namesake of the “mini-Neptunes” or “sub-Neptunes”: planets larger than Earth and smaller than Neptune. According to NASA they are the most common type of exoplanet observed so far in our galaxy, yet the Solar System has no such planet. Why this is so is one of the open questions of research.

Moons: Neptune has 16 known moons. The largest is Triton, 2,700 km in diameter, discovered by William Lassell on 10 October 1846, only 17 days after the planet itself. Triton is the only large moon in the Solar System that orbits its planet in the retrograde direction, opposite to the planet's rotation; it is probably a Kuiper Belt object captured by Neptune. Its surface is about −235 °C cold.

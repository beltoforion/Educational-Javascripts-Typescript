TOI-6894 b: a giant planet around a very small star

The red dwarf TOI-6894 has only about 20 percent of the Sun's mass and is the lowest-mass star known to host a transiting giant planet. The planet TOI-6894 b is slightly larger than Saturn but has only about half its mass.

According to the leading theories of planet formation, such a planet should not exist around so small a star. It was found in a search of TESS data on more than 91,000 low-mass red dwarfs and with observations at ESO's Very Large Telescope. Because such stars are very common, the finding has implications for the estimated number of giant planets in the Milky Way.

Source: University of Warwick: "Discovery of giant planet orbiting tiny star challenges theories on planet formation.", 2025-06-04
